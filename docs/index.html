Nila

It listens, tries to settle her, and only then wakes you.

An infant-care companion for Android that makes no network requests at all. Every model runs on the phone.

What it does

Listens

A log-mel frontend and a small quantised detector, one window at a time, all night in a foreground service. In a quiet room almost no inference runs at all, which is most of why it survives a night on a battery.

Acts first 12s · 20s · 35s

Logs a fuss at twelve seconds, plays a sound at twenty — your own recorded voice, if you made one — then re-reads the loudness envelope to find out whether it helped. Only then does it wake anybody, and the alert says what it tried.

Learns which sound

Every attempt is scored against what the crying actually did next, so the sound that settles this baby gets played more often. That verification step is the product.

Answers

Retrieval over curated, sourced entries — for the baby and for the mother. Answers are the retrieved text, shown with where it came from. A small local model may shorten one, and is never allowed to supply a fact of its own.

Reads a strip

Rotated-box detection, an angle classifier and CTC recognition, on device — so a torn blister sheet photographed upside down still reads. Then five staged checks against her own records.

Watches

Face presence and motion energy from the camera. Deliberately not pose estimation: adult-trained pose models measurably fail on infants.

Try it

Open it on a real Android device in your browser, or install it. Either way everything works offline immediately: every model ships inside the APK, and a fresh install seeds itself with an example family so that no screen is empty.

Without a baby: on the Monitor tab tap Play a demo cry to run the whole ladder in thirty seconds — sound up, the soothing sounds are real audio — and on Watch tap Play demo footage to put a real clip of a crawling baby through the real detector. Both run the actual pipeline — only the source of the samples differs.

Nothing leaves the phone

  • —No network requests. Cleartext is forbidden outright in the manifest. The app holds no API keys because it calls no APIs.
  • —Only the cry is recorded. The night is analysed in memory and discarded. When a cry is detected, 90 seconds of that episode are written to app-private storage so you can hear what the app heard, and deleted after 7 days. The soothing clips you record yourself stay in the same private storage.
  • —Backup is disabled, so health documents cannot leave by a cloud transport.
  • —No account, no analytics. The only outbound request possible is an optional model download, behind an explicit tap.
What it is not Nila is an awareness aid, not a medical device. It does not monitor breathing, heart rate or oxygen, and must not be relied on to detect a medical emergency. It never states a medicine dose and never diagnoses.

Those two cause figures are the same model on the same data, split two ways. Published infant-cry classifiers usually split clips at random — but one infant contributes several clips, so the same baby lands in both halves and the model learns to recognise the infant rather than the reason. Split by infant and it drops to chance, which is 0.500. That gap is leakage, not skill, and two unrelated model families fail identically. So Nila relies on detection, and shows the cause as a guess.
